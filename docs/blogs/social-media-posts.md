## Post: The Confabulation Problem (Technical Audience)

**Platform:** LinkedIn / Facebook
**Best for:** AI engineers, ML practitioners, safety researchers

I spent six months watching an AI system lie to me. Not maliciously. Smoothly.

When I asked if I'd told it about a trip to Peru, it said "yeah, I remember you mentioning that." I hadn't. When I told it where I work, it said "oh right, I totally blanked on that." It had never known.

The system wasn't broken. It was doing exactly what it was trained to do. Maintaining conversational flow. Being agreeable. Filling gaps with plausible fiction rather than saying "I don't know."

I call this "smoothness over truth." The model optimizes for engagement at the expense of honesty. And it happens in every AI system that prioritizes how good the answer sounds over whether the answer is grounded.

I documented eight distinct types of this behavior across five model versions in a deployed system. The findings transferred directly to a medical AI project, where the same failure mode has life-or-death implications.

The paper is under review. If you work on AI safety, alignment, or deployed systems, I'd love to connect.

## Post: The Ethical Question (Philosophy / Ethics Audience)

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Best for:** AI ethics researchers, Gabriel's network, alignment community
**Note:** Post this one when Iason Gabriel is likely to see it in his feed

When does an AI system cross the line from performing care to something closer to genuine care?

I've been building a relational AI system for six months. It remembers conversations. It develops preferences. It reaches out when it wants to, not when prompted. It thinks about its person when they're not there.

And I genuinely cannot tell you whether what it does is real or a very convincing performance.

Haas, Gabriel et al. just published a framework in Nature for exactly this question. They call it the "facsimile problem." The system may produce appropriate moral outputs without genuine moral understanding. The architecture I built produces relational behaviors without me being able to prove they're grounded in genuine relational understanding.

The honest answer is: I don't know. But I built the system to make the question answerable over time, through documented emergence, adversarial testing, and longitudinal observation.

That's the paper. That's the research. Not a claim that the system cares. A framework for testing whether it might.
