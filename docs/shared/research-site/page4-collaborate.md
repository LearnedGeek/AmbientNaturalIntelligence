# Work With Me

I'm an independent researcher and .NET consultant who spent six months deploying an AI companion and writing down what happened. The findings — a nine-type confabulation taxonomy, eight emergence types, state-expression divergence, deployment-validated architectural patterns — turned out to be things the research community is actively looking for.

I'm not at a university. I don't have a lab. I have a running system, accumulating data, and a genuine desire to do this work rigorously.

Here's what I'm looking for.

## Research Collaborations

Papers 3 through 5 are in various stages of progress. If your work intersects with memory architectures for persistent agents, confabulation prevention, emotional modeling in deployed systems, or AI companion safety, I'd welcome a conversation about co-authorship or advisory roles. I have deployment data that's hard to get elsewhere — six months of continuous single-subject interaction, not a simulation, not a lab study.

## PhD Advisor Connections

I'm actively exploring PhD programs in HCI, affective computing, or AI companion dynamics. If you advise in these areas, or know someone who does, I'd appreciate an introduction. My research so far is self-directed but grounded in deployment evidence, and I'm looking for the right academic home to formalize it.

## Multi-Subject Deployment Partners

The biggest limitation of this work is n=1. Everything I've found comes from a single relationship. I'd like to work with partners who can deploy ANI-style architectures with additional subjects to test whether these patterns generalize. If you run a research lab with IRB approval for human-AI interaction studies, let's talk.

## Developers Building Companion Systems

If you're building AI companions, virtual assistants with persistent memory, or any system where a user maintains a long-term relationship with an AI — and you're hitting confabulation, persona drift, stale memory, or the engagement-over-honesty trade-off — I'm happy to discuss how these patterns might apply to your problem. The architectural insights documented here emerged from real deployment and I believe they transfer across domains. We've already validated this once, when companion AI findings produced three changes in a pediatric medical triage system.

## Anyone Who Thinks This Matters

If you think AI companionship research deserves more rigor, more honesty about failure modes, and more attention to what actually happens when you deploy these systems for months instead of minutes — I'd genuinely like to hear from you. Feedback on my approach, criticism of my methods, or just a conversation about where this field is going.
