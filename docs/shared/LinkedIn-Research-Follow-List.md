## Strategy Before the List

**Follow vs. Connect.** For strangers, *follow* is the right move — it's a one-click action that doesn't require acceptance, signals interest without being presumptuous, and still causes their public posts to appear in your feed. *Connect* is for people you have a real reason to know (already corresponded with, shared mutual connections, attended the same event). Sending connect requests to strangers with "hi, I like your work" is the academic equivalent of a cold email with weaker signal.

**Engagement is asymmetric.** Following alone doesn't move the needle much. What reshapes your feed AND creates reciprocal visibility is engagement — liking posts, commenting on substance (never generic encouragement), and posting your own work. A thoughtful comment on a researcher's post that references a specific finding in their paper is worth 100 likes. Don't comment on everything; comment when you have something real to say.

**Posting matters more than following.** When you post about Paper 2 progress, a specific finding, or a research question, people who follow YOU see it. Following researchers increases the chance THEY see your posts (because the algorithm weights "content from people you follow" higher in their feed). So this list is bidirectional — it's not just "who Mark reads," it's "who might start reading Mark back."

**Don't burn day one.** If you follow 25 people in a single session and like 10 posts each, it looks performative. Spread it out — follow five per day, engage organically, let the feed evolve over a week. The LinkedIn algorithm is more generous to accounts with sustained engagement patterns than spike-and-disappear patterns.

**Disambiguation strategy.** For common names, LinkedIn search by name alone returns too much. Three reliable filters that narrow it down:
1. **Add the institution:** `"Kristina Lerman" Indiana University`
2. **Add a paper title keyword:** `"Sharma" sycophancy` (easier than remembering first names)
3. **Add a research area:** `"Liu" proactive conversational agents`

When multiple candidates match, check the profile photo + affiliation + publication list in the "About" section. Most researchers list their top papers in their bio.

## Strategic Engagement Sequence

**Week 1 (this week):**
- Follow Tier 1 (5 people). Don't engage yet — just let the feed start showing their posts.
- Connect (not follow) with Kristina Lerman specifically. She's the only one where you have a legitimate connect reason.

**Week 2:**
- Follow Tier 2 (10-12 people). By now your Tier 1 feed should be populating. Like 2-3 posts from Tier 1 people if the posts are substantive.
- Post something of your own — maybe a brief note about Paper 2 progress, or a specific architectural finding (the Apr 13 architecture-over-training principle would be a good standalone post). Tag no one. Let the feed do its work.

**Week 3:**
- Follow Tier 3 (IU Luddy faculty). If you're planning to formally apply, following them now establishes your presence in their feed before any formal application lands.
- Comment on one substantive post from Tier 1 — preferably engaging the actual research content, not just saying "great paper!" Example: if Lerman posts a new finding, comment with the specific architectural question it raises from the deployment side.

**Week 4+:**
- Continue posting your own work at ~1 per week cadence. Engagement should start becoming organic as your feed shifts.
- Follow Tier 4 as bandwidth allows.

**What not to do:**
- Don't mass-follow in a single session (algorithmically suspect; also performative)
- Don't comment on posts just to be seen (you WILL be seen; the algorithm rewards presence)
- Don't DM strangers asking for feedback on your paper (that's what cold email is for)
- Don't post about ANI in a way that sounds like a pitch. Post the research findings as research findings. The interest comes from substance, not framing.
